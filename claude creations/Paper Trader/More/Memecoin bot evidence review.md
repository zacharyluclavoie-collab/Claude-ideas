# Cutting memecoin bot losses beats chasing signals

No published evidence shows that any entry signal for new or trending DEX tokens predicts positive returns after costs, so a paper-trading bot's win rate is more likely to improve by avoiding losses than by finding better entries. Most launches die fast. About 1.4% of pump.fun tokens graduate to a DEX, and roughly 69-79% stop trading on launch day. Rug and honeypot prevalence depends on definition: 3.6% of new tokens by Chainalysis' narrow test, 66% by GoPlus' "suspected" label on Base, and 98% by Solidus' broad pattern match. The tokens that do run are disproportionately manipulated: 82.8% of tokens that gained more than 100% in one cross-chain study showed manipulation. Execution costs fall hardest on small retail bots: snipers hit a third to over half of new Solana tokens in the first block, and measured Solana sandwiches average roughly $7-15 per attack. That is a large share of a $50-500 position. The best-supported structural rules are a small fixed loss per position, no fixed profit cap, a ratcheting trailing stop, sizing on worst-case (total) loss, and a cap on aggregate open risk. Strategy validation needs forward-recorded data with no survivorship, time-ordered walk-forward testing, and a count of every configuration tried. One caveat applies throughout: the research team could not open most primary documents, so nearly every figure below comes from search summaries or secondary coverage and should be checked before it is quoted.

## Most launches die within a day, and the survivors are mostly manipulated

The headline rug-pull rates are not comparable, because each one defines "rug" differently. Solidus Labs reported that about **98.6% of pump.fun tokens** (January 2024 to March 2025) showed rug-pull or pump-and-dump characteristics, and that only about 97,000 of more than 7 million kept at least $1,000 of liquidity ([CoinDesk](https://hotfix.coindesk.com/business/2025/05/07/98-of-tokens-on-pump-fun-have-been-rug-pulls-or-an-act-of-fraud-new-report-says)). Pump.fun publicly disputed the findings. The liquidity-survival figure of under 1.4% is more defensible than the "fraud" framing, because it measures an outcome and not intent. Chainalysis' 2024 data put rug-pull characteristics at only **3.59% of newly minted tokens** ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/crypto-crime-2024-2025-chainalysis)), though the definition behind that number was not found. GoPlus counted 381,790 suspected scam ERC-20s among 573,484 new Base tokens, about 66.6% ([GoPlus deep dive](https://defihacklabs.substack.com/p/goplus-security-deep-dive-on-how)). A 2021 Uniswap V2 study labelled about 97.7% of its sample malicious using a liquidity-pull heuristic ([arXiv 2201.07220](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.07220)). The sensible reading is that outright honeypots are a minority, but almost nothing keeps its liquidity or price. No BSC-specific 2025 share was found. GoPlus counts show BSC honeypots falling to about 60 by March 2025, while Base rose to about 1,000 ([GoPlus](https://blog.gopluslabs.io/2025/03/14/financing/2025-03-14-SOLANA-BASE-BNB-Token-Issuance-Trend-Report/)). Those are counts, not shares.

Survival numbers are more consistent. The long-run graduation rate on pump.fun is about **1.4%** ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/pump-fun-memecoins-are-dying-at-record-rates-less-than-1-survive)), with weekly readings as high as 2.01% in March 2026 ([KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/pump-fun-meme-coin-graduation-rate-hits-2-01-highest-since-july-2025)). CoinGecko found **68.67%** of tokens stop trading on launch day ([Blockchain.News](https://blockchain.news/news/pump-fun-memecoins-lifespan-study)), and Bitquery put the figure at 79% ([KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/solana-memecoin-frenzy-leaves-79-of-tokens-stopped-after-day-one)). The gap comes from defining survival as "last trade". Two things the notes could not supply matter for a bot: no sourced distribution of 2x, 5x or 10x outcomes, and no 30-minute or 1-hour survival rates for any chain. Neither can be assumed. Those distributions have to be recorded by the bot itself.

The most important base-rate finding for signal design is that winners are suspicious. In a 34,988-token study across Ethereum, BSC, Solana and Base, 707 tokens gained more than 100%, and **82.8% of them showed manipulation**, including recurrent wash trading ([arXiv 2507.01963](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01963v2)). Wash trading is a minority of coins overall (4% to 8% by conservative tests, per [SBFC 2025](https://sbfc.sydney.edu.au/2025/papers/SBFC2025_4G3_P154.pdf) and [CoinPaprika](https://coinpaprika.com/ru/education/launchpad-red-flags/)), but it is concentrated among coins that attract volume. A "volume and momentum" screen therefore selects heavily for engineered activity. A related study found that the pump.fun outcomes it examined were driven by trader behavior, coordination and attention rather than fundamentals ([SBFC 2025](https://sbfc.sydney.edu.au/2025/papers/SBFC2025_4G3_P154.pdf)), though the notes did not confirm this is the paper behind that claim.

## Snipers and sandwiches take a large bite from small positions

On Solana, a retail bot buying seconds after launch is almost certainly buying after a sniper cohort that is about to leave. Bitquery-based work found an outside buy in the first block for **32% of new coins** (down from 42% as blocks sped up), and about 52-53% within roughly 1.3 seconds ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/solana-study-finds-rapid-pump-fun-buying-persisted-as-blocks-sped-up/)). ChainCatcher found over 50% of tokens sniped in the creation block ([ChainCatcher](https://www.chaincatcher.com/en/article/2185070)). Pine Analytics reported that snipers sell **85% of their holdings within five minutes** ([Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines)). Sniper-prevalence figures conflict by definition (Pine's own coverage ranges from about 50% of launches to 1.75%), so the direction is reliable and the magnitude is not. Nobody has measured the later buyer's P&L. That the later buyer is exit liquidity is a well-motivated inference and not a result.

Sandwich losses are measurable on Solana and Ethereum and unmeasured on Base and BSC. A study of Jito data over about four months of early 2025 counted 521,903 sandwiches costing users more than $7.7M ([IMC'26 paper](https://cnitarot.github.io/papers/imc26_solana.pdf)), and MEV Monitor counted 87,061 in one April 2025 week extracting about $620k ([MEV Monitor](https://mirror.xyz/0x7c855e1bF411Ab5975235bC8C74E032615073044/mwh7Bkqp_nmsOkW6T7ee4tqXyXYgaRwLQd8zi38HbcY)). Dividing totals by counts gives roughly **$7-15 per Solana attack**, against about **$140 per attack** for Ethereum private-channel sandwiches ([arXiv 2512.17602](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.17602)). These are averages per attack, not per trade, and the share of swaps that get sandwiched was not found. Still, a $7-15 hit on a $100 position is a 7-15% loss on one leg, far above Solana network fees of about $0.001-0.05 per swap ([Madeonsol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/solana-priority-fees-guide)). Network costs are negligible next to sandwiching, DEX fees and price impact. The best controllable lever is slippage tolerance: Bitquery found Jupiter trades allowing 20-50% slippage were sandwiched 14 times as often as those under 3%, at the price of more failed transactions ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/solana-sandwich-bots-took-29-7-million-before-costs-bitquery-estimates/)). That evidence is observational. App choice also matters: a 2026 study reported much higher victimization for several popular trading front-ends than for Jupiter Ultra ([CryptoBriefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/jupiter-ultra-sandwich-attacks-study/)), and the same paper shows protected order flow is not immune.

Other chains look different, though evidence is thin. A 2026 study found no economically meaningful sustained sandwiching on rollups and argued naive heuristics overstate it ([arXiv 2601.19570](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19570v1)), so Base's main execution risk is more likely honeypots and rugs than sandwiches. For BSC the only claims found were self-interested forum posts, and Ethereum private routing via Flashbots Protect hides transactions but does not eliminate private-channel sandwiches ([Flashbots](https://docs.flashbots.net/flashbots-protect/overview)). Solana transaction reliability is also poor for bots: one study found a **58.43% failure rate** for bot transactions ([arXiv 2504.18055](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18055v1)), which means a paper bot that assumes every order fills is materially optimistic. No independent median tip, landing rate or all-in round-trip cost was found for any chain.

## Scanner and signal evidence supports filtering, not selecting winners

The detection literature supports using features to avoid disasters, and nothing in it supports using them to pick winners. A TON study detected rug pulls within five minutes of trading with AUC up to 0.891 using liquidity-based labels ([arXiv 2509.01168](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01168)). A Solana study found idle-time signals work better on pump.fun and TVL signals better on Raydium ([arXiv 2608.20271](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.20271)). Those signals are largely reactive, since the rug is already under way, so they suit exit triggers better than entry selection. A paper warned that many detectors suffer temporal leakage, with features taken after the drain has begun ([arXiv 2603.11324](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.11324)). Holder concentration is the most consistently cited pre-event feature, with one paper reporting Gini above 0.8 before rugs versus below 0.5 for normal projects ([arXiv 2201.07220](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.07220)). Those cutoffs come from a single 2021 Uniswap study and are unvalidated. Bundling can also hide true concentration. The same paper reported that about 90% of tokens using locking contracts eventually became rug pulls or malicious, a search-summary figure that undercuts "liquidity locked" as a safety signal.

Third-party scanners should be treated as weak evidence. The only independent measurements found concern GoPlus: it flagged about **60% of ground-truth trapdoor tokens** on Uniswap ([arXiv 2309.04700](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.04700)), and in a Telegram bait-and-trap study it labelled 138 honeypots where the authors' tool found 1,492 ([arXiv 2610.10149](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.10149)). Roughly 40% of traps slip through, and malicious logic can appear after the scan. No independent accuracy figures exist for RugCheck, TokenSniffer or De.Fi, partly because vendors do not publish datasets ([TM-RugPull](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21529v1)). A scanner pass should be a necessary condition and never a sufficient one, and the bot's own pre-trade simulated sell is stronger evidence than any vendor flag.

For return prediction, the honest result is a null. No study tested any signal (buyer-seller ratio, holder growth, volume acceleration, deployer history, social metrics) against forward returns after costs for new tokens. Social evidence is concurrent: abnormal tweets accompany abnormal returns, with delayed selling after the dump ([arXiv 2306.02148](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.02148v1)), which is not a tradable lead. The adjacent literature on established small coins points to short-horizon reversal and not momentum ([IRFA 2021](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v78y2021ics1057521921002349.html)), but those samples are CEX-listed coins at day-plus horizons, and reversal gains in illiquid tokens would likely be consumed by spreads and rug risk. Copy-trading leaderboards are contaminated: of 3,170 wallets with $100k+ gains, only 26% of the gain total passed conventional-trading checks, and 15 of the top 25 gains met wash-loop criteria ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/pump-funs-biggest-winners-may-be-impossible-to-copy-as-752-million-study-exposes-hidden-trading-gap/)). Wallet-level outcomes are poor: 99.6% of pump.fun traders had not realized over $10,000 ([CoinMarketCap Academy](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/nl/article/996percent-of-pumpfun-traders-have-not-realized-over-dollar10000-in-profits-data-shows)). No independent audit of a profitable commercial sniper or copy bot was found. A paper bot that appears to win should be presumed to be exploiting an unrealistic fill model until shown otherwise.

## Exits and sizing carry the most defensible evidence, all of it borrowed

Exit and sizing evidence is the strongest in the notes, but none of it is from memecoin backtests. For lottery-type stocks, which have frequent small losses and occasional big rallies, stop-loss rules trim losses while keeping exposure to the rallies and improved risk-adjusted returns ([Dai et al., Global Finance Journal 2023](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/glofin/v56y2023ics1044028322000503.html)). Trend-following on liquid crypto used a trailing stop that only ratchets upward and reported net-of-fee Sharpe above 1.5 on a rotating top-20 portfolio ([Swiss Finance Institute RP 25-80](https://ideas.repec.org/p/chf/rpseri/rp2580.html)), though the stop details were not verified. Together these support one structure: a small fixed initial loss, no fixed profit cap, and a trailing stop, perhaps after scaling out a minority. The implication is a win rate well below 50% judged by expectancy and payoff ratio. A memecoin write-up found tighter take-profit and stop bands gave higher win rates but smaller average returns ([KetchupJL](https://ketchupjl.github.io/backtesting)), consistent with that trade-off but unverified and with unclear cost treatment. A direct consequence for the user's goal is that "win rate" is the wrong target. Raising it by tightening targets would likely cut the tail that pays for the losses.

A stop on an illiquid new token is a request, not a guarantee. Gaps, failed sells and vanished liquidity mean the realized loss can exceed the nominal stop, so position size should be set on worst-case loss (possibly 100%). A fixed-fractional rule risking about 0.25-1% of equity per position on that basis is a reasoned default, not a measured optimum. Kelly theory agrees that edge uncertainty argues for betting well below full Kelly ([MacLean, Thorp and Ziemba](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/157/Papers/Good_Bad_Kelly.pdf)). Because no edge has been demonstrated, Kelly cannot be computed here. Concurrent positions are not independent: memecoins correlated about 0.82 with DOGE in 2024 ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/why-doge-wif-other-memecoins-are-crashing)) and fell about 73% from their late-2024 peak versus 54% for BTC ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/markets/memecoins-sell-off-as-bitcoin-price-takes-the-spotlight-is-meme-season-over)), though those figures cover established coins and not fresh launches. Total open risk should therefore be capped as one correlated bet. No source gives an optimal position count, and no evidence supports forcing a minimum number of open positions. Barber and Odean show higher trading activity goes with lower net returns for retail investors ([Berkeley](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/Papers%20current%20versions/Individual_Investor_Performance_Final.pdf)), and equity evidence suggests average lottery-like entries have negative expectancy ([NBER w12936](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w12936/w12936.pdf)). A minimum-position quota is defensible only as a tightly capped data-collection experiment. For regime filters, BTC trend filters cut drawdowns in large-cap backtests ([Quantified Strategies](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/?p=1192563), a secondary blog), but no source tests them on new memecoins, none supports daily loss limits as performance enhancers, and one analysis found an altcoin moving-average strategy still suffered a 98% drawdown ([Quant Hedge](https://quanthedge.substack.com/p/your-new-best-friend-in-crypto-trading)). Circuit breakers are cheap insurance against bugs and rug clusters, justified by risk theory, not by measured effect.

## Validation must replace survivorship and optimism with recorded reality

The dominant backtest bias is survivorship, and the notes found no estimate of how much it inflates DEX returns. The fix is to record every new pool at birth, including those that later rug, from pool-creation events and not from "currently trending" listings. Mutable fields such as holder counts, LP lock status, mint authority and scanner flags should be stored as timestamped snapshots and used only if `fetched_at` precedes the decision time. Dead pools need a terminal state, and exits on vanished liquidity should be modelled as total loss or the last executable price and not the last quote. Fills should be computed against pool reserves at the bot's size plus fees, tips, non-landing and sandwich loss. The time-based detail matters because some API caches are stale: GeckoTerminal changed some endpoints from 30- to 15-minute updates ([CoinGecko changelog](https://docs.coingecko.com/changelog?page=1)). These recommendations are the research team's reasoning, not tested results. The notes also found no peer-reviewed paper-versus-live gap for DEX bots, so any assumed haircut is an unknown to be measured.

Statistical discipline matters because memecoin payoffs are skewed and fat-tailed. Bailey and Lopez de Prado's Deflated Sharpe Ratio corrects for selection across many trials ([SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)), and the Probability of Backtest Overfitting uses combinatorial cross-validation ([SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253)). The notes' own unverified rough estimates put a per-trade Sharpe of 0.1 at roughly 270 or more independent trades to reach significance, and 0.05 at over 1,000. These should be re-derived from the primary sources. A workable protocol is to freeze the universe and features, run time-ordered walk-forward splits with an embargo at least as long as the maximum holding time plus feature lookback, tune only on training windows, log the number of configurations tried, and bootstrap results by token and by day since launches in the same hour share regime. Then use an untouched holdout, live paper, and finally small live trading used to measure fills. A "shadow quote" (a Jupiter quote logged at each paper decision) gives real executable prices without trading. For storage, on-chain swap events from an RPC or webhook provider (for example Helius) give exact history, while free API tiers are only good for polling at modest scale. Quoted limits conflict across pages: GeckoTerminal about 30 calls/minute ([FAQ](https://apiguide.geckoterminal.com/faq)), Helius with differing credit and request figures between pages ([Helius](https://helius.dev/docs/billing/rate-limits)), and Dune's free plan now view-only ([Dune](https://docs.dune.com/learning/how-tos/pricing-faqs)). Limits should be verified at the provider before building.

## Prioritized changes the evidence supports

The ordering below puts changes that remove a bias or a known loss first, and changes that depend on unproven edge last. The "evidence" column says how much each rests on measured data.

| Priority | Change | Why | Evidence strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Replace mid-price or last-trade fills with a pool-reserve fill model including DEX fee, price impact, priority fee and tip, a non-landing probability and a sandwich-loss draw. Log a Jupiter shadow quote at each paper decision. | Network fees are trivial but sandwiches average roughly $7-15 on Solana, bot transaction failure was 58%, and no paper-versus-live gap is documented, so paper P&L is an upper bound. | Moderate for the cost sizes, weak for exact parameters |
| 2 | Record the full launch universe forward from pool-creation events, with timestamped snapshots and terminal states for dead pools, and stop sampling from trending lists. | Survivorship is the dominant bias, and only about 1-2% of launches graduate. | Strong on the bias, none on its magnitude |
| 3 | Size each position on worst-case (total) loss at about 0.25-1% of equity, and cap total open risk as one correlated bet. | Rug and honeypot outcomes are common and correlation rises in crashes. | Theory plus indirect evidence; the exact percentages are a reasoned default |
| 4 | Stop optimizing win rate. Use a small fixed loss, no fixed profit cap, a ratcheting trailing stop, and optionally a small scale-out, and judge by expectancy and payoff ratio. | Evidence from lottery-type stocks and liquid-crypto trend-following favors this shape, and tight targets raise win rate by cutting the tail. | Borrowed from other assets; unverified on memecoins |
| 5 | Add hard pre-trade filters: a simulated sell, mint and freeze authority checks, and holder concentration with the unvalidated Gini and top-holder cutoffs treated as starting points. Treat any scanner pass as necessary and never sufficient. Use liquidity-drain and idle-time signals as exit triggers. | GoPlus missed about 40% of traps, concentration is the most consistent feature, and drain signals are reactive. | Moderate for filtering, weak for cutoffs |
| 6 | Do not enter on early volume or momentum alone, and avoid the first blocks. Consider a tight slippage cap, since high slippage tolerance was sandwiched 14 times as often. | Sniper cohorts exit within minutes and 82.8% of tokens that rose over 100% showed manipulation, so volume is a poor discriminator. | Moderate on snipers and manipulation, observational on slippage |
| 7 | Remove any minimum-open-positions rule, or confine it to a strictly capped scout budget used only to collect data. | No evidence supports quotas, and overtrading harms retail returns. | Indirect, from equities |
| 8 | Validate with time-ordered walk-forward testing and an embargo, a count of all configurations tried, a Deflated Sharpe and overfitting estimate, token- and day-clustered bootstrap intervals, an untouched holdout, then live paper, then minimum-size live trading to fit slippage and landing models. | Skewed, fat-tailed payoffs and many tried configurations make in-sample results unreliable. | Method evidence is strong, but formulas and sample sizes need re-derivation |
| 9 | Add a daily loss limit, drawdown shutdown and optional BTC/ETH regime filter as circuit breakers, and test each rather than assuming it helps. | They protect against bugs and correlated crashes, but no memecoin evidence shows they improve returns. | Weak |

## Conclusion

The central finding is that no source in this review demonstrates a positive-expectancy, after-cost strategy for new memecoins, and the best-documented participants are snipers who sell within minutes. A paper bot's results therefore reflect its fill model more than its signals. Improving "win rate" by filtering rugs and by modelling costs is realistic. Improving expectancy by entry signals is not yet supported by any evidence, and a bot that appears to win on paper should be assumed to be exploiting unrealistic fills until shown otherwise.

The practical shift is from searching for a better predictor to building a measurement instrument. Recording the full launch universe, logging every quote and fill, and fitting slippage and landing models would give the first honest estimate of the payoff distribution that Kelly sizing, stop calibration and significance tests all need, and that the literature does not provide. Several open questions would change these conclusions: the real shares of 2x, 5x and 10x outcomes at 30 minutes and 1 hour, sandwich frequency and loss on Base and BSC, and whether any signal survives costs out of sample. This evidence is also time-bound, since 2026 data show graduation rates ranging from 0.26% to 2.01% and sandwich activity changing with validator bans and app changes. The primary documents behind most numbers were not read in this review, so verify any figure before relying on it.
