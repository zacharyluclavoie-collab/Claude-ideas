# Filter harder, trade wider, stop tightening targets

An automated DEX paper-trading bot can raise its win rate and its trade count together only in one way: widen the universe it watches, and apply filters that remove trades with negative drift (rugs, dead launches, faked volume, entries at the graduation top). Tightening take-profits or stops does not do this. It moves win rate up or down while expectancy stays the same or falls, and DEX costs make every extra round trip more expensive. The cost hurdle is the central fact. A realistic DEX round trip costs about **3-4%** once LP fees, bot fees and slippage are added, before sandwich losses and failed transactions. Nearly every short-horizon predictor with a measured size is an order of magnitude smaller than that. Examples are 15-minute reversal (about 1.3 bp gross), next-day reversal after abnormal volume (about 0.32% per standard deviation) and intraday momentum. Most "high trade count, positive expectancy" methods used by systematic traders therefore fail for a small DEX taker. Mean reversion on liquid tokens, grids and cross-DEX arbitrage either lose to costs or belong to a handful of professional searchers. The filters with real support are survival filters: a minimum number of unique early traders, not buying the graduation event, bundle-adjusted concentration, wash-trade screens, and a contract and sell-simulation pass treated as hygiene. They improve the odds that a position is still alive when the exit system acts, but no study shows they produce positive returns. For a paper bot, the cheapest way to get "more trades" is to log a shadow trade for every candidate it sees, so it can learn which filters work without spending capital or adding cost. One caveat applies throughout: researchers could read only search-engine snippets and abstracts, because page fetches were blocked. Almost every number below is second-hand and should be checked against the primary source before anyone builds on it.

## A 3-4% round trip sets the bar every rule must clear

Start with the arithmetic, because it decides which ideas are worth testing. Take a 0.3% LP fee, a 1% Telegram-style bot fee and about 0.5% slippage per side. The round trip comes to roughly **3.6%**, so a strategy with a 50% win rate needs its average win to be about 3.6 points larger than its average loss just to break even. That figure is the research team's own sum, built from the fees documented in earlier notes: Trojan and BONKbot charge about 1% per trade ([Skool](https://skool.com/crypto/trojan-vs-bonkbot-telegram-trading-bots), forum-sourced). Thin new pools add price impact even for small buys ([EPJ Data Science / arXiv 2502.10512](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.10512)). Sandwiches on Solana cost roughly **$7-15 per attack**, derived from the totals in a Jito-data study ([IMC'26](https://cnitarot.github.io/papers/imc26_solana.pdf)). That is 7-15% of a $100 position on one leg. Bot transactions failed **58.43%** of the time in a large Solana study ([arXiv 2504.18055](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18055v1)), so a paper bot that assumes every order fills is optimistic. One lever that reduces this drag has observational support. Jupiter trades allowing 20-50% slippage were sandwiched about **14 times as often** as trades capped under 3%, although the tighter cap means more failed fills ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/solana-sandwich-bots-took-29-7-million-before-costs-bitquery-estimates/)).

Against that hurdle, the documented short-horizon effects are small. Abnormal volume predicts lower next-day returns, with a Fama-MacBeth slope of -0.102 (t = -4.50). Where short-selling is restricted, which describes almost every DEX token, a 1-SD volume spike lowers the next-day return by **0.319%** ([Garfinkel et al., Financial Management 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/fima.12491)). A matched out-of-sample study found significant 15-minute reversal in **90% of 183 Binance pairs** (against 2.7% of US stocks) in every year since 2021. The gross edge peaked near **1.3 bp per trade against a 5 bp round-trip cost** ([arXiv 2608.21888](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.21888)), so the effect is real but cannot pay a CEX taker, let alone a DEX taker paying a hundred times more. This changes the shape of the trade-count question. Fixed costs hit every trade, while gross edge per trade usually shrinks as signals are loosened to fire more often. **Loosening entry criteria to trade more is the most reliable way to make a DEX bot lose faster.** More trades are worth having only if each one keeps its edge per trade, which means the extra trades have to come from more candidates, not lower standards.

## Survival filters have evidence; return filters do not

The strongest new entry evidence concerns which launches stay alive, not which ones go up. A pump.fun survival analysis covered **352,621 launches** in October 2026. It found 57% stop trading within 5 minutes, 75% are dead within 1 hour, 89% within 24 hours and 97% within 7 days. Only 2.5% ever graduate, and 88% of those graduate inside the first hour. The strongest predictor was unique traders in the first 5 minutes (hazard ratio 0.77 per SD). Tokens with **21 or more unique traders had a median remaining life of about 18 hours, versus about 40 minutes** for those with 20 or fewer. The model's out-of-sample AUC for death within 24 hours was only **0.67**, and "Mayhem Mode" launches were 81% dead within an hour ([GitHub mahad1z/memecoin-survival](https://github.com/mahad1z/memecoin-survival), a personal project but the only document read in full). This is the best candidate for a minimum-activity gate. It buys the exit system hours instead of minutes, but it does not show that surviving tokens make money.

Graduation itself is a bad entry. Across all launchpads with a graduation feed from September to November 2026, only **26.4% of 50,749 graduates** were at or above their graduation market cap one hour later, and about **8.8% after 24 hours**. The median pump.fun graduate had lost about 96% by 24 hours ([MemeFees](https://memefees.com/research/memecoin-survival-2026)). That figure comes from a search summary and the 8.8% could not be corroborated, so confidence is low to medium. The direction matches an earlier finding: **82.6-82.8% of tokens that gained over 100%** in a 34,988-token cross-chain study showed manipulation indicators ([arXiv 2507.01963](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01963v2)). Graduation-rate spikes from launchpad incentives, such as a jump to 6.7% on one day after BOOST changes, say nothing about whether value is retained ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/defi/2026-07-29-pump-fun-token-graduation-rate-jumps-boost-changes-launch-incentives-409815)).

### Hidden concentration matters; raw top-10 share does not

The common practitioner rule "skip if the top 10 holders own more than 30-40%" ([defade.org](https://defade.org/blog/how-to-spot-solana-rug-pull), vendor blog) is not supported by the only survival model found. In that model, higher top-10 concentration was mildly *protective* (HR 0.90). A creator selling in the first 5 minutes raised the death hazard by only about 12% after controlling for demand ([memecoin-survival](https://github.com/mahad1z/memecoin-survival)). The signal that separates risky from safe launches is concentration hidden by bundling. In a dataset of 40,000+ migrated Solana launches, merging bundled accounts raised the measured top-10 share by about **24 points for high-risk launches versus about 6 points for low-risk ones** ([MELT/MemeTrans, arXiv 2602.13480](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480v2)). Clustering creator addresses raises the top 1% of creators' share of all coins from 38.89% to 58.57%, which shows how much single-wallet views miss ([Meme Coin Factories, arXiv 2609.10246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10246)). Single-token forensics fit this picture: 82% of LIBRA sat in one cluster, and about 40% of Ava AI sat in 23 deployer-linked wallets ([Bubblemaps](https://bubblemaps.io/case-studies), [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bubblemaps-ava-ai-token-sniping-deployer-wallets-solana)). No population crash rate exists for these clusters. A "bundle gap over about 15 points" rule interpolates between the two reported means and has not been validated.

### Contract checks are hygiene, not edge

Locks and authorities protect against only a small share of rugs. Of 117 manually verified Solana rugs, **76% were pump-and-dumps, 22% liquidity withdrawals and only about 2% freeze-authority abuse** ([arXiv 2603.24625](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24625)). On pump.fun-style launchpads, authorities are revoked and liquidity is handled by the protocol, so these checks pass trivially and carry no information. They matter for direct Raydium, Meteora or Orca pools and for EVM tokens. No locked-versus-unlocked rug-rate comparison exists. One 2021 Uniswap study found about 90% of tokens using locking contracts eventually turned malicious ([arXiv 2201.07220](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.07220)), and the frequently repeated "Chainalysis" claim about locked liquidity could not be traced to Chainalysis. Scanner passes are weak: GoPlus caught only about **60% of ground-truth trapdoor tokens** ([arXiv 2309.04700](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.04700)). Since pump-and-dump is the dominant rug mode, holder and flow filters do more work than contract filters.

### Wash trading is common enough to filter on

At least **17% of pump.fun transactions** ([arXiv 2609.10246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10246)) and **21.4% of 200M Solana memecoin transactions** ([arXiv 2602.13480v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480v1)) are wash trades, concentrated in the tokens that attract volume. The detectable patterns are a buy and a sell inside one transaction, self-loops, matched pairs and circular flows ([BSC early-warning paper, arXiv 2603.13830](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13830)). Bitquery documented one token faking over $500k of volume in 12 hours from a few wallets, which it caught through trades per trader and the share of wallets trading both ways ([Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/blog/solana-volume-numbers-are-a-lie)). Funding-link graphs alone "are not a wash-trading detector" ([arXiv 2611.11436](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11436)). Unique traders are harder to fake than volume, but not impossible. In 166,098 launches, 1,012 persistent sniper rings raised early buyer counts by **16.1%** while raising SOL inflow by only **6.3%**, an effect statistically indistinguishable from zero ([arXiv 2607.02795](https://ideas.repec.org/p/arx/papers/2607.02795.html)). A minimum-trader gate should therefore count unique *funded* wallet clusters and be cross-checked against net inflow. No study measures how much a wash filter changes the winner or rug share.

### Smart money adds little beyond the curve state

Wallet-following evidence is weak to negative. Early-buyer "fast-wallet" teams were associated with a 2-4x higher chance of the bonding curve reaching 20 SOL, but with **no excess chance of graduation**. Their hold-out precision was 0.5-0.75, and many signals arrived after the curve already held 20 SOL, adding nothing beyond the curve's own state ([arXiv 2610.39458](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39458)). A retrospective team-following simulation rejected selection-matched null models in only **3 of 40 tests** after Holm adjustment, despite a funding graph with look-ahead ([arXiv 2611.11436](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11436)). Leaderboards are contaminated too: only 26% of $100k+ wallet gains passed checks for conventional trading ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/pump-funs-biggest-winners-may-be-impossible-to-copy-as-752-million-study-exposes-hidden-trading-gap/)). Any wallet signal has to beat a baseline that uses curve state alone, with point-in-time wallet labels and real signal-to-fill delay.

## Exits change win rate easily and expectancy rarely

Theory sets the rules here. Kaminski and Lo show that under random-walk returns a stop-loss always *lowers* expected return. A stop adds return only when losers keep losing (momentum). In their monthly equity data, some stop rules added about 50-100 bps per month during stopped-out periods ([MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/114876/Lo_When%20Do%20Stop-Loss.pdf)). The crypto evidence splits the same way. Stops layered on monthly momentum portfolios across 147 coins (2015-2022) raised returns, Sharpe ratios and alpha in every market state ([Sadaqat and Butt, JBEF 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2214635023000473)). A 30% threshold is mentioned, but the best threshold could not be read. Industry stops on plain long coin positions roughly **halved both returns and volatility**, and fewer than 35% of long positions survived without being stopped out ([Białkowski, Economics Letters 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165176519304227)). That is risk reduction, not added edge. New DEX tokens that rug or bleed out plausibly behave like the momentum case, where losers keep losing, so a hard stop is more likely to help there than on liquid pairs. That is an inference, and a rug gaps straight through any stop. Realised losses will exceed the nominal stop, so positions should be sized on a total loss.

Tight trailing stops look harmful on crypto's noise. Practitioner backtests, all low confidence, point the same way. A 3% trailing stop on 15-minute bars cut win rate by **8-12 points** compared with fixed stops ([TrendRider](https://trendrider.net/blog/stop-loss-strategies-crypto-trading-2026)). A 1% trailing stop cut one year's result from **+318.5% to +143.3%** ([ArrowAlgo](https://arrowalgo.com/what-4000-backtests-taught-us/)). A trailing stop earned about 8% less than a fixed -3.5% stop on one bot ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/trendrider/i-tested-4-stop-loss-methods-on-my-crypto-bot-heres-what-actually-works-47i0)). On a DEX the cost arithmetic adds to this. A 3% stop on a 3.6% round trip means each stop-out loses more to fees than to price. Stop and trail distances must be wide relative to both volatility and cost. No source gives an optimal width in ATR multiples for DEX tokens.

Scaling out and time stops are essentially untested. No peer-reviewed crypto study of partial take-profit exists. The one new-token study with fixed sell thresholds entered Uniswap V2 tokens at the 60th swap. It reported positive profit across the remaining tokens only *after* excluding honeypots and rug pulls, which cannot be done ex ante. Many tokens showed "an illusion of huge profits" early ([EPJ Data Science](https://epjds.epj.org/articles/epjdata/abs/2026/01/13688_2025_Article_602/13688_2025_Article_602.html)). The timing evidence does support short time stops on signal-driven entries. Intraday jumps continue to the end of the day but not into the next day ([Springer, Digital Finance](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42521-024-00116-1)). Twitter-promoted pump run-ups reverse, starting at least 30 minutes after the dump phase ([IRFA](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1057521924004113)). Abnormal attention comes with same-day gains and later reversal ([Pugachyov, FoFI 2024](http://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-148-Nikolay-Pugachyov.pdf)). A position bought on a spike and still flat after a few hours has used up the window in which the documented effect works. Closing it frees capital for another candidate, which is one honest way to add trades. Partial take-profit is the opposite case. It raises win rate mechanically, and given the extreme right skew of new-token returns, selling most of a position early likely cuts the tail that pays for the losers. It should be limited to a minority of the position.

The barrier arithmetic explains why. For a driftless price with stop distance SL and target distance TP, the probability of hitting the target first is about SL / (SL + TP). Shrinking TP raises win rate by exactly enough to keep gross expectancy at zero, and the fixed round-trip cost turns it negative. This is a standard identity, not a sourced result. In a worked example, a 40% win rate with a $250 average win and $100 average loss makes +$40 per trade, while a 60% win rate with a $100 win and $200 loss makes -$20 ([ActionForex](https://www.actionforex.com/?p=26232)). **A rule raises win rate without lowering expectancy only when it changes the conditional drift of the trades taken.** Removing rugs and dead launches qualifies. Moving the exit barriers does not.

## The methods quant traders use for many positive trades mostly fail at DEX costs

Short-horizon signals do have measurable predictive power, but it varies with liquidity and fades fast. Liquid coins show continuation. ETH continues significantly for up to 30 minutes after large positive moves, while BTC shows no significant predictability at any horizon tested ([MDPI JRFM 19(9):692](https://www.mdpi.com/1911-8074/19/9/692)). Bitcoin's first half-hour (in volume time) predicts its last half-hour ([Shen, Urquhart and Wang](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/100181)). Small, illiquid coins show daily reversal driven by low volume ([Fičura 2023](https://ideas.repec.org/p/prg/jnlwps/v5y2023id5.003.html), [Zaremba et al. 2021](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v78y2021ics1057521921002349.html)). Order-flow imbalance Granger-causes Bitcoin returns ([Springer RQFA](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11156-023-01148-1)), but it decays within seconds to minutes, and a DEX bot sees swaps only after they land in a block. Explicit social buy calls predict short-term moves, especially for low-cap coins after recent losses ([Electronic Markets 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12525-025-00815-6)), but the size of the effect was not visible. Every one of these was measured on CEX data. None was re-estimated on DEX swaps with DEX costs.

### Mean reversion pays the liquidity provider, not the taker

Short-term reversal is a liquidity-provision premium, concentrated in low-volume pairs and tied to inventory risk and adverse selection ([CERGE-EI WP 730](https://www.cerge-ei.cz/pdf/wp/Wp730.pdf)). Once costs are included, the abnormal reversal returns become "very small and statistically insignificant" ([Brunel](https://www.brunel.ac.uk/economics-finance-and-accounting/research/pdf/1801-Jan-GMC-Price-Overreactions-In-The-Cryptocurrency-Market.pdf)). A Bitcoin buy-the-low reversal strategy earned 98.43% a year in sample and **failed out of sample**, while the breakout version held up ([Quantpedia revisit](https://quantpedia.com/revisiting-trend-following-and-mean-reversion-strategies-in-bitcoin/?a=6080)). Pairs trading results are mixed to negative once costs are counted ([arXiv 2109.10662](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.10662), [IEEE Access](https://doaj.org/article/8fbfa5ffeaae44a5a4c635f23f5ca05c)). A DEX taker cannot collect this premium. The only seller of reversal is whoever supplies liquidity, and on AMMs that is the LP, who also loses to arbitrage. In most large Uniswap pools studied across 2022-2023, fees fell short of loss-versus-rebalancing ([arXiv 2404.05803](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.05803v2)).

### Grids report high win rates and hide trend losses

A static grid has an expected return of roughly zero under a random walk before fees. A 2025 dynamic-grid paper beat buy-and-hold on BTC and ETH from 2021 to 2024, mainly by cutting drawdown (about 50% against an 80% ETH fall), but it **assumed zero fees** ([arXiv 2506.11921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11921v1)). Retail evidence is anecdotal and points both ways: a SUI grid at -33.31% with a 45.59% drawdown, a BTC grid earning +7.35% while BTC rose 14.15%, and grids stranded about 23% under water when price trended out of range ([Binance Square](https://www.binance.com/square/hashtag/GridBot)). The best of 576 SOL grid configurations earned +11.7% while SOL fell 37%, a textbook case of selection bias ([DEV.to](https://dev.to/tatelyman/python-async-grid-trading-bot-for-solana-architecture-and-backtest-results-57b)). Hummingbot liquidity miners describe pure market making as "roughly break-even" before incentives ([Hummingbot blog](https://hummingbot.org/blog/interview-with-liquidity-miner-dominator008/)). A grid "wins" almost every completed cycle, so its win rate is meaningless. The losses sit in inventory, and its true P&L is mark-to-market against simply holding that inventory. On a DEX, grid spacing must be several multiples of the 0.6-2%+ round-trip cost. That leaves few cycles on anything except volatile, range-bound tokens, which are the tokens most likely to trend out of range or rug.

### Cross-DEX dislocations belong to searchers

Arbitrage between DEXs and between CEX and DEX is the textbook high-count, high-win-rate strategy, and the profits are concentrated. On Ethereum from August 2023 to March 2025, **19 searchers** made $233.8M from 7.2M CEX-DEX arbitrages, and the top three took about 75% ([arXiv 2507.13023](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.13023v2)). Solana arbitrage averaged about **$1.58 profit** across roughly 90M successful trades in a year ([Helius](https://www.helius.dev/blog/solana-mev-report)), and an earlier Jito figure had 98% of attempts failing ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2023/02/28/solanas-spam-problems-persist-despite-tech-improvements-mev-researchers-say)). Cross-exchange spreads have "decreased greatly" since April 2018 ([Paderborn TAF WP 067](https://wiwi.uni-paderborn.de/fileadmin-wiwi/cetar/TAF_Working_Paper_Series/TAF_WP_067_CrepellierePelsterZeisberger2022_rev.pdf)). A paper bot that polls quotes and sees a 1% gap between two pools is almost always looking at a stale quote, a gap it cannot fill at size, or one a builder-connected searcher closes in the same block. A paper fill model will "capture" these gaps, and that is a bug, not an edge.

### Regime filters trade count for quality

No cost-adjusted study measures what a BTC trend filter does to memecoin or new-token trading. The evidence is indirect. A BTC-plus-altcoin trend strategy cut maximum drawdown to about 19% against over 80% for buy-and-hold ([Quantified Strategies](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/which-trading-strategy-is-best-for-crypto-bitcoin-vs-altcoin/), vendor blog). Crypto momentum premia flip sign between regimes, strongly positive in the COVID recovery and negative in the 2022 crash ([AAU thesis](https://projekter.aau.dk/projekter/files/839386480/master_thesis_final2026.pdf)). A parameter sweep found Sharpe at its best around 40-50-day SMAs, declining smoothly beyond that, the kind of plateau that indicates robustness ([QuantConnect forum](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/21195)). The cost of the filter is opportunity: about **84% of Binance spot tokens** were below their 200-day average in one reading ([coinotag](https://en.coinotag.com/bitcoin-outperforms-84-percent-altcoins-below-200-day-average)), so a long filter keeps an altcoin bot out of the market most of the time. A regime filter is best understood as a governor. It removes the trades most likely to lose in bear phases and so plausibly raises win rate and expectancy on the trades left, but it reduces trade count. Results should be reported per regime, because a pooled 2021-2022 average can describe neither regime.

## Statistical honesty decides whether any of this is real

Trade count matters for a second reason: precision. The standard error of a win rate is sqrt(p(1-p)/n). At 55%, the 95% interval is about ±9.8 points at 100 trades, ±4.9 at 400 and ±3.1 at 1,000. Telling 55% apart from 50% therefore takes roughly 400 trades. Showing that expectancy is positive needs about 4 / (per-trade mean/sd)² trades, which is about 400 at a per-trade Sharpe of 0.1 and 1,600 at 0.05. Skewed memecoin payoffs push both numbers higher. These are standard statistics computed by the research team. Multiple testing is the larger danger. With five years of daily data, trying more than about 45 independent configurations nearly guarantees an in-sample Sharpe of 1 or more while the expected out-of-sample Sharpe is zero ([Bailey et al.](https://carmamaths.org/jon/BODT.pdf)). The Probability of Backtest Overfitting ([SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253)) and the Deflated Sharpe Ratio ([SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)) exist to correct for this. Every filter threshold in this report is one more trial.

This is where a paper bot has an advantage a live bot lacks. It can record a **shadow trade for every candidate**: each token that passes the base universe, along with the filter values at decision time and the outcome under each exit rule. That multiplies the learning sample at no cost and lets each filter be judged on the trades it rejects as well as the trades it takes. The right acceptance test for a new rule follows from the barrier arithmetic. Expectancy per trade after costs must not fall, and **total expectancy (expectancy × number of trades) must rise**. A filter that halves trade count has to more than double edge per trade to pay its way. A rule that only raises win rate fails this test. Breadth helps in the same way. Many small equal-sized positions make realised results converge toward the true mean, and naive equal weighting survives costs in crypto ([Nottingham](https://nottingham-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/4739516)). That helps only if the mean is positive. If it is negative, which is what most memecoin evidence suggests, more positions simply make the loss more certain. Small coins also crash together, so breadth diversifies the upside, not the drawdown.

## Prioritized rules to test

The list puts cost realism first, then rules that remove negative-drift trades, then exits, then ideas to drop. "Confidence" means confidence that the rule moves expectancy in the stated direction on DEX data, not confidence that the bot will be profitable. No source shows any configuration is profitable after DEX costs.

| # | Rule (testable form) | Expected effect | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Charge every paper fill a pool-reserve price impact plus LP fee plus bot fee (about 3.6% round trip as a default), a sandwich-loss draw of about $7-15 per Solana leg, and a non-landing probability fitted to logged attempts. Cap slippage tolerance near 3%. | Removes phantom edge; lowers reported win rate and expectancy to honest levels | High that it is necessary; parameters medium |
| 2 | Log a shadow trade for every candidate that enters the base universe, with point-in-time features. Evaluate rules on at least 400 trades, count every variant tried, and require deflated-Sharpe or PBO checks. | Large learning sample at zero cost; guards against false filters | High |
| 3 | Never buy on the graduation or migration event. Require at least 30-60 minutes after graduation before considering an entry. | Removes an entry with about 26% odds of being above water at +1h | Medium-low (snippet data, not corroborated) |
| 4 | Require 21 or more unique traders in the first 5 minutes (or a time-scaled equivalent), counting funded wallet clusters, with net inflow not lagging buyer growth. | Higher survival (median life about 18h versus 40 min); higher win rate if survival carries into returns | Medium on survival, low on returns |
| 5 | Skip if bundle-adjusted top-10 share exceeds raw top-10 share by more than about 15 points, or if any funder cluster holds more than 20-30%. Do not use raw top-10 share above 30% as a skip rule. | Removes hidden-insider launches; avoids a filter that the only survival model contradicts | Low (thresholds interpolated) |
| 6 | Skip if same-transaction buy+sell or two-way-wallet volume exceeds about 20% of volume, or if the top 3 wallets carry most of the volume. | Removes faked volume, which is concentrated in the tokens a volume screen selects | Low-medium (prevalence solid, filter effect unmeasured) |
| 7 | Treat mint/freeze authority, honeypot sell simulation and LP status as hard pass/fail hygiene and never as a positive score. Scanner pass is necessary, not sufficient. | Removes a small slice of rugs (about 2% of verified Solana rugs are authority abuse; GoPlus misses about 40% of traps) | Medium |
| 8 | Do not enter on a volume or attention spike with an overnight hold. If an entry is spike-driven, apply a time stop: exit if not at least +1x round-trip cost (about +4%) within 2-4 hours. | Avoids the documented next-day reversal; recycles capital into more trades | Medium on direction (CEX evidence), low on the window |
| 9 | Initial hard stop of about 20-35% for fresh tokens, sized so a total loss costs 0.25-1% of equity. No trailing stop tighter than about 25%, and the trail activates only after roughly +50%. | Cuts bleeding losers where losers keep losing; avoids noise stop-outs that each cost a full round trip | Low-medium (direction from theory and crypto momentum studies; widths are judgement) |
| 10 | Limit partial take-profit to at most about one-third of the position (for example at 2x). Leave the rest on the trailing stop with no fixed cap. | Modest win-rate gain without cutting the right tail | Low (untested) |
| 11 | Gate new entries on BTC above its 50-100-day SMA and report results per regime. Tune the length only on a plateau. | Fewer trades; plausibly higher expectancy per trade in bear phases | Low-medium |
| 12 | Wallet-following only as a hypothesis: the signal must fire before the curve holds about 20 SOL, must use point-in-time labels and real delay, and must beat a curve-state-only baseline. | Most likely no incremental value | Low |
| 13 | Drop as taker strategies: 15-minute mean reversion on liquid tokens, cross-DEX or CEX-DEX gap capture, and grids with spacing under about 3x the round-trip cost. | Avoids strategies whose measured gross edge is below DEX costs or owned by searchers | High that they fail after DEX costs |

## Conclusion

The two goals in the question are less in conflict than they look, but only if "trade count" is redefined. Win rate and expectancy rise together when the bot refuses trades with negative drift, and trade count can rise at the same time if the bot watches more launches, more launchpads and more chains rather than lowering its bar. What does not work is the familiar retail toolkit: tighter targets, tighter trails, faster flips, grids and arbitrage. Each produces a pleasing win rate while the cost of each round trip, larger on a DEX than almost any measured signal, quietly erodes expectancy. The evidence also changes what a filter is for. The best-supported filters predict *survival*, not *returns*, so their value depends on an exit system that turns extra hours of life into realised gains, and no study has yet tested that link.

The practical implication is that the bot's most valuable output in the near term is a dataset, not P&L. A shadow log of every candidate, with point-in-time bundle gaps, unique-trader counts, wash shares and outcomes under several exit rules, would answer questions the literature leaves open: whether survival filters carry into returns, where stop widths sit on the cost curve, and whether a partial take-profit costs more tail than it saves. Until that log shows positive total expectancy after full costs over hundreds of shadow trades, the safest assumption remains that a winning paper bot is benefiting from its fill model.
