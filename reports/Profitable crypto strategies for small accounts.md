# Small crypto accounts profit by surviving, not predicting

For a $1k-$10k automated account, the only strategies with documented positive expectancy are slow, low-turnover, cost-aware ones: trend following on BTC/ETH-class coins, basis or funding carry (when the spread is wide), and plain staking or lending. All three earn modest, regime-dependent returns, and none is a path to large gains at this size. Arbitrage, market making, copy trading and memecoin sniping are won by professionals or by the platforms, and the independent evidence shows retail participants in them losing. The best-sourced result of the review is negative: Uniswap v3 fees failed to cover arbitrage losses in most large pools, 97% of persistent Brazilian futures day traders lost money, and Hyperliquid front-end (mostly retail) traders lost $1.11B in one quarter while market makers gained. The positive evidence is weaker than it looks. Published trend Sharpes above 1.5 come from 2018-2024, when BTC rose from about $3k to over $100k, and the strongest figures come from single-author papers with thin cost modelling. A realistic planning range for a validated bot is **0-2% per month on average, with 20-40% drawdowns and multi-month losing streaks**. That range is my judgement, not a sourced figure. The most useful thing a paper-trading bot can do is test whether a slow trend or carry rule beats buy-and-hold or T-bills after costs on recorded data. It is not a way to find a hidden entry signal. Confidence caveat: every researcher could read only search-result summaries because page fetches were blocked, so almost every number below is second-hand and should be checked against its primary source before use.

## Professionals own arbitrage, market making and wallet-copying

The measured profit pools in on-chain trading sit with a handful of well-resourced players. On Ethereum, 19 searchers extracted about **$233.8M from 7.2M CEX-DEX arbitrages** between August 2023 and March 2025, roughly $32 per trade, and the top three captured about three-quarters of volume and value ([arXiv 2507.13023](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.13023v3)). Two of the three dominant block builders run their own integrated searchers, which gives them an informational and ordering edge that a small account cannot replicate. CEX-DEX arbitrage also requires exchange inventory and builder relationships. On Solana, Jito data show about 90.4M successful arbitrages in a year at an **average profit of $1.58** ([Helius](https://www.helius.dev/blog/solana-mev-report)), and an earlier Jito-sourced figure had 98% of arbitrage attempts failing because duplicate-spam ordering rewards ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2023/02/28/solanas-spam-problems-persist-despite-tech-improvements-mev-researchers-say)). The two figures come from different periods, so the combination is an inference, but a $1.58 average win against heavy failed-transaction cost is not a retail business. I found no independent study of a profitable small-account arbitrage bot.

Market making splits into two cases. For passive DEX liquidity provision there is real academic evidence, and it is negative. In Uniswap v2/v3 pools studied across 2022-2023, fee income fell short of arbitrage losses (loss-versus-rebalancing) in most of the largest pools, and v2 pools beat their v3 counterparts for passive LPs ([arXiv 2404.05803](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.05803v2)). An older Bancor-commissioned study (a conflicted source, using 2021 data) found only 3 of 17 v3 pools earned fees above impermanent loss and **49.5% of LPs had negative returns** ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/half-of-uniswap-liquidity-providers-are-losing-money)). The theory behind this is that a constant-function LP position is effectively short volatility and gets picked off by arbitrageurs at stale prices ([Milionis et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.06046)). Counter-evidence exists: some less-traded pools were profitable, v3 papers argue profits are possible with active management ([arXiv 2111.09192](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.09192)), and one blog found the top-earning LPs used narrow ranges and short holds ([Mirror](https://mirror.xyz/0xA3DB621697fBC0dA0b16160eb8E201E1a763D2c1/SI9A-eRE8g7gy0oDbqChl1mOm6JwBriimDq29VE4O58)), but that rests on one non-peer-reviewed post. Gas on rebalancing eats a $1k position quickly, so the plausible niche is correlated or stable pairs at low returns. For CEX market making, the evidence is promotional or anecdotal. Hummingbot's own beginner material says a $10,000 account can generate millions of monthly volume, so fee tier decides the outcome ([Hummingbot](https://docs.hummingbot.org/academy-content/top-beginner-misconceptions-about-market-making/)). The viral "$6,800 into $1.5M" story is an outlier whose own article says it needed speed, capital and deep exchange access ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/explained/this-trader-turned-68k-into-15m-by-using-a-high-risk-strategy-heres-how)). Retail-versus-professional outcomes on a perpetuals venue make the point: over Mar-Jun 2025 on Hyperliquid, front-end traders lost **$1.11B net while market makers gained $208M** and algorithmic takers roughly broke even (one venue, about three months) ([Jia et al., AFT 2026](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.AFT.2026.36)).

Copy trading fails at the transmission step. Bitquery reviewed 3,170 wallet-token positions with gains of at least $100,000 and found only about a quarter of the gains passed checks for conventional market trading. Many involved tokens that arrived by transfer, so a follower could never replicate the purchase ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/pump-funs-biggest-winners-may-be-impossible-to-copy-as-752-million-study-exposes-hidden-trading-gap/)). Bitquery sells data and the figure comes from press coverage, so treat it as moderate confidence. A simulation-based paper reports smart-money wallets averaging 14% per trade while copiers capture about 3% under realistic frictions, and that bots manipulate prices to induce predictable copying ([arXiv 2601.08641](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v2)). Against that, GMGN-style tools charge about 1% per transaction ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/pump-funs-biggest-winners-may-be-impossible-to-copy-as-752-million-study-exposes-hidden-trading-gap/)), and Telegram bot users have lost custody funds: about $520k across BONKbot and Solareum in March 2024 ([Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/telegram-bots-bonkbot-and-solareum-users-suffer-520000-losses-due-to-hacks/)). No study measures real follower P&L, so the verdict is an inference from structure: later entry, 1% fees, thin pools and leaderboards selected by past winners.

Airdrop farming is the one activity where retail participants plausibly earned money, but it is not an automated-trading strategy and the data are poor. An academic study of nine major airdrops found up to **95% of proceeds were sold via exchanges soon after distribution**, which the authors read as benefiting farmers ([arXiv 2312.02752](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02752)). Projects now filter hard: Linea and Nansen flagged 516,960 of 1.3M eligible wallets as sybils ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/335979/)). Per-wallet averages of $9,384 exist, but the method was unseen and likely survivor-biased ([Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/experts-assess-incomes-of-airdrop-hunters/)). A secondhand blog reports a median net profit of $47 after gas and time against a mean of $1,850 ([Chainscore](https://www.chainscorelabs.com/blog/airdrop-strategies-and-community-building/airdrop-analytics-and-measurement/why-most-airdrop-roi-calculations-are-fundamentally-flawed)). I rate that figure low confidence, though the implied shape, a heavy-tailed lottery with a near-zero median, is the common one. There is no sourced per-hour or per-dollar return, no 2025-26 data, and a paper-trading bot cannot validate it.

## Memecoin bots lose before the signal matters

The earlier memecoin evidence review (built from search summaries too) concluded that nothing published shows an entry signal for new tokens beating costs, and the new notes strengthen that. Wallet-level outcomes on pump.fun are poor: a CoinGecko analysis found most active traders lost money each month from April 2024 to late 2025, with the profitable share rarely above 50% and a low of 30.1% in June 2025 ([CoinGecko Research](https://www.coingecko.com/research/publications/pump-fun-traders-are-making-a-comeback)). Over 99% of wallets never realised more than $10,000 and only about 293 made over $1M ([CoinMarketCap Academy](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/nl/article/996percent-of-pumpfun-traders-have-not-realized-over-dollar10000-in-profits-data-shows)). Pump.fun's founder disputes the accounting because unrealised gains and post-graduation trades are excluded, and profitable shares swung by month (one report claims over 73% in April 2026 ([BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/pump-fun-traders-profit-comeback-meme-coin-season/))). "Roughly 50-70% of wallets lose, with profit concentrated in a tiny tail" is the defensible summary. A bot should do worse than the wallet median, because it trades more, pays more fees and fails more transactions. No audited bot P&L dataset exists in the notes.

The loss mechanisms are structural. Pine Analytics found deployers fund sniper wallets that buy in the launch block, and that **85% of sniper holdings are sold within five minutes**, so later retail buyers supply the exit ([Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines)). Only about 0.7-0.8% of pump.fun launches graduated in mid-2025 ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/how-pump-fun-captured-80-of-solana-memecoins-and-can-it-last)), and in a 34,988-token cross-chain study **82.8% of tokens that gained over 100% showed manipulation** ([arXiv 2507.01963](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01963v2)), so a volume-and-momentum screen selects for engineered activity. Transactions fail often: bots had a 58.43% failure rate in a study of 1.5B failed Solana transactions ([arXiv 2504.18055](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18055v1)). Trojan and BONKbot charge about 1% per trade, a 2% round-trip hurdle (forum-sourced, unverified) ([Skool](https://skool.com/crypto/trojan-vs-bonkbot-telegram-trading-bots)). Sandwiches cost roughly $7-15 per attack on Solana (my arithmetic from [IMC'26](https://cnitarot.github.io/papers/imc26_solana.pdf) totals), which is minor for a $1,000 trade but material at $50-100.

The mitigations with any measured support are on the execution side. Bitquery found Jupiter trades allowing 20-50% slippage were sandwiched 14 times as often as those under 3% ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/solana-sandwich-bots-took-29-7-million-before-costs-bitquery-estimates/)), observational and at the cost of more failed transactions. A 2026 protected-order-flow study reports Jupiter Ultra's excess victimization ratio at 0.7 versus above 10 for several Telegram and terminal front ends, though the authors also say protections are weaker than users expect ([CryptoBriefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/jupiter-ultra-sandwich-attacks-study/)). More than 86% of single-transaction Jito bundles lacked tips high enough for reliable priority ([IMC'26](https://cnitarot.github.io/papers/imc26_solana.pdf)). Scanner checks are necessary but weak: GoPlus caught about 60% of known trapdoor tokens ([arXiv 2309.04700](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.04700)). I found no controlled study of liquidity or age filters, exit rules or position caps for memecoins. Those remain untested design judgements, and any paper bot that appears to win should be assumed to benefit from an unrealistic fill model.

## Slow trend and carry on liquid coins are the credible candidates

Trend following on BTC and large coins has the most supporting literature, with heavy caveats. Zarattini, Pagani and Barbon combine several Donchian lookbacks with volatility-based sizing and a trailing stop, and report net-of-fee **Sharpe above 1.5 and 10.8% annualised alpha versus Bitcoin** on a rotating top-20 liquid-coin portfolio ([SSRN 5209907 via RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/p/chf/rpseri/rp2580.html)). I saw only the abstract-level summary, with no confirmed drawdown, CAGR or fee assumption. Hudson and Urquhart report break-even costs well above typical crypto costs ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10479-019-03357-1)), and Fieberg et al. find a multi-horizon trend factor persists in large liquid coins and survives costs ([JFQA](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/trend-factor-for-the-cross-section-of-cryptocurrency-returns/4C1509ACBA33D5DCAF0AC24379148178)). Against that, a walk-forward test of moving-average rules on Bitcoin 2011-2019 found no attractive predictable Sharpe when training on one year and testing on the next ([Glasgow](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/302711/1/302711.pdf)), a decade-of-evidence paper found parameter fragility with negligible assumed costs ([arXiv 2009.12155](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.12155)), and a retail-site backtest of a single 20/10 Donchian rule on BTC from January 2022 to June 2026 shows **Sharpe 0.38, 38.5% drawdown and 25 trades** ([CoinQuant](https://www.coinquant.ai/strategies/btc-donchian-channel-3d-backtest), low-confidence aggregator). The practical reading is that the value of trend on majors is mostly drawdown control (a WisdomTree in-sample test shows max drawdown -64% versus -83.4% for BTC ([WisdomTree](https://www.wisdomtree.com/dk/insights/blog/enhancing-bitcoin-returns-using-momentum)), and issuer marketing should be discounted). Alpha over buy-and-hold is unproven out of sample. The 2018-2024 window is a single secular up-move, so my estimate for forward BTC-only trend Sharpe is 0.5-1.0, a judgement and not a sourced number. Costs bite short-lookback and alt-heavy baskets (Monash reports short lookbacks give high Sharpe that frequent rebalancing erodes ([Monash](https://www.monash.edu/business/mcfs/our-research/all-projects/investment-strategy/trend-following-strategies-for-crypto-investors))). The 2026 AdaptiveTrend preprint reports Sharpe 2.41 with a -12.7% drawdown ([arXiv 2602.11708](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11708v1)), but it is a single-author, unreplicated 2022-2024 result and should not be a target.

Momentum and mean reversion in cross-section are weaker for a small spot account. Liu, Tsyvinski and Wu document a momentum factor in crypto ([Journal of Finance](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:77:y:2022:i:2:p:1133-1177)), but another study finds time-series momentum strong and cross-sectional momentum weak, with significant returns turning insignificant after costs in large liquid coins ([AUT/ACFR](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/918729/Time_Series_and_Cross_Sectional_Momentum_in_the_Cryptocurrency_Market_with_IA.pdf), snippet only). Horizon matters: one study finds momentum to 2-4 weeks and reversal beyond a month ([HSE](https://spb.hse.ru/en/news/559533243.html), secondary), while others report reversal at 1-4 weeks, so sources conflict. Riksbank work places short-term reversal profits mainly in low-activity pairs ([Riksbank WP 413](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/rapporter/working-papers/2022/no.-413-trading-volume-and-liquidity-provision-in-cryptocurreny-markets.pdf)), where spreads and rug risk would absorb them. Part of the published premium comes from short positions that a spot-only account cannot take. I found no verified net figure for long-only top-50 momentum or reversal, and no post-2022 decay or multiple-testing study. Mean reversion on liquid majors therefore has no documented cost-adjusted edge, and I would treat it as an experiment, not a plan.

Carry is the best-documented yield-like trade, but it has compressed. Funding and futures premiums ran 20-40% annualised in early 2024 and 3-5% later ([Cointelegraph/Talos](https://cointelegraph.com/markets/sygnum-bitcoin-carry-trades-2027)), CME basis was around 20-25% in November 2024 ([CME OpenMarkets](https://www.cmegroup.com/openmarkets/equity-index/2025/Spot-ETFs-Give-Rise-to-Crypto-Basis-Trading.html)), and by January 2026 it was about 4.7% with the trade described as unwinding against a risk-free rate near 4.5% ([Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2026/01/22/wall-street-pulls-back-bitcoin-trade)). At those levels the excess over T-bills is a few percent before fees and transfer costs, which is thin for a $1k-$10k account split across two venues (my inference, with no sourced minimum-capital figure). It is also not risk-free: He and Manela note funding arbitrage carries risk beyond margin and costs ([arXiv 2212.06888](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.06888v5)), and in the 10 October 2025 cascade some of the best-hedged shorts were cut by exchange auto-deleveraging while USDe traded far below par on Binance ([FTI Consulting](https://www.fticonsulting.com/insights/articles/crypto-crash-october-2025-leverage-met-liquidity), [Galaxy](https://www.galaxy.com/insights/research/cryptos-flash-crash-liquidation-binance-adl-auto-deleveraging)). Staking and lending are cheaper still in effort: 2026 figures reported ETH staking about 2.5-4.5%, SOL about 3.7-6% and Aave USDC around 2-5% with occasional anomalous spikes ([Dextools](https://www.dextools.io/news/how-to-stake-ethereum-complete-staking-guide-2026), [CoinStats](https://coinstats.app/news/7e7f54c59908780e9fd397fd4a23b2a5200beb1b00fcba08fb89627be2fd6aee_Ethereum-vs-Solana-Staking-in-2026-Yields-Rewards-and-Risks-Compared/), [Aavescan](https://aavescan.com/rates)). That is roughly a T-bill proxy plus smart-contract, depeg and slashing risk, and on $1,000 gas decides whether it is worth doing on Ethereum mainnet.

## Realistic returns, sizing and validation standards

The retail base rates are discouraging and nearly all come from non-crypto or discretionary trading. Of Brazilian futures day traders who persisted more than 300 days, **97% lost money** and only 1.1% earned more than the minimum wage ([SSRN 3423101](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101)). In Taiwan, fewer than 1% of day traders reliably earned positive net returns ([Barber et al.](https://www.escholarship.org/content/qt7k75v0qx/qt7k75v0qx.pdf?t=ohrd1z)), with roughly two-thirds of individual losses attributed to transaction costs per a secondary write-up ([Berkeley Haas](https://newsroom.haas.berkeley.edu/individual-investors-see-red-prof-terry-odean-finds/)). I found no retail algorithmic-bot survival study, so the crypto-specific evidence is the Hyperliquid result above plus a 2020 Binance figure that 79% of customers used leverage over 20x ([NYU Stern](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/assets/documents/Duron-Carielo_Is%20There%20A%20Future%20In%20Perpetual%20Futures.pdf)). Even professionals show how much of crypto return is beta: the HFR Cryptocurrency Index returned 65.8% in 2023 and 59.8% in 2024, and about 51.4% annualised over five years to February 2025 ([HFR](https://www.hfr.com/?p=38140)), yet Bloomberg's headline was that crypto hedge funds failed to beat bitcoin in 2024 ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-01-15/crypto-hedge-funds-had-a-great-2024-but-failed-to-beat-bitcoin)). Cambridge Associates' head of digital assets says to expect a 50% drawdown ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bstm0ttbd36wmby0uy2o/portfolio/crypto-hedge-funds-deliver-bitcoin-like-returns-without-the-volatility), excerpt only). The setting for a small account is therefore different: 1% a month on $1,000 is $10, and the benchmarks to beat are buy-and-hold BTC/ETH and a roughly 4% risk-free yield.

The arithmetic of drawdowns drives sizing. These are standard identities I computed, not sourced findings: a 20% loss needs a 25% gain, 33% needs 50%, 50% needs 100%, and at a 3% monthly net return a 50% drawdown takes about 24 months to recover (about 70 months at 1%). Theory favours betting a fraction of Kelly because estimation error in the edge is the main danger, and full Kelly implies a roughly 50% chance of a 50% drawdown (a search-snippet figure of the standard result) ([MacLean, Thorp and Ziemba](https://escholarship.org/content/qt5mr5k8qj/qt5mr5k8qj.pdf), [PMC9955835](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9955835)). Fractional Kelly is a formal shrinkage of an uncertain edge ([arXiv 2503.17927](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2503.17927)). Volatility scaling raised Sharpe in the original equity study ([NBER w22208](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22208)) but replications are mixed ([Cederburg et al.](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf)), and none are crypto-specific. My practical suggestion, a judgement and not a sourced result, is fixed-fractional risk of about 0.25-1% of equity per position measured at worst-case loss, a volatility-scaled gross exposure cap, and treating Kelly as an upper bound. Circuit breakers have no measured crypto evidence and should be tested as risk controls and not assumed to add return. Overtrading is the documented retail failure channel, so turnover itself is a cost to minimise.

Validation is where a paper bot earns its keep. In the Quantopian study of 888 strategies, more backtesting effort meant a larger in-sample to out-of-sample gap, and in-sample Sharpe explained less than 25% of out-of-sample variation ([Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/quantopians-academic-paper-about-in-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-trading-alg/)). The Deflated Sharpe Ratio and Minimum Track Record Length address multiple testing and fat tails ([SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)). No source gives paper-to-live crypto slippage numbers, so measured live fills must replace assumed haircuts. Carry and lending rates can be backtested from public histories such as Glassnode funding spreads, Aavescan and DefiLlama, while ADL, depeg and exploit events can only be stress-tested ([Glassnode](https://studio.glassnode.com/charts/c7693b87-3cc1-462b-73b5-4098bf17175b), [Aavescan](https://aavescan.com/rates)). LP backtests need pool swap, mint and burn events plus CEX prices and gas. Only forward paper trading with logged quotes can show real execution cost.

## What a paper-trading bot can honestly test

Candidates are ranked by how well the evidence and testability line up, and the confidence levels refer to whether a positive net result is plausible, not to whether it will occur.

| Candidate | What the evidence says | Realistic expectation at $1k-$10k | Validatable by paper bot on real data? | Confidence it can beat the benchmark |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ensemble trend (multi-lookback Donchian or MA, vol-sized, long/flat) on BTC/ETH/SOL | Literature mostly positive, but fragile, in-sample on one bull run, costs lightly modelled | Drawdown cut versus buy-and-hold; alpha uncertain; forward Sharpe about 0.5-1.0 (my estimate) | Yes: free daily and hourly OHLCV, fees known, walk-forward and deflated Sharpe feasible; years of data are limited in independent trials | Moderate-low on beating BTC return; moderate on lowering drawdown |
| Funding or basis carry (BTC/ETH) | Real but compressed to near T-bills in 2025-26; tail risks from ADL, depeg and counterparty | A few percent annualised above risk-free in good regimes; spikes in bull phases | Partly: funding and basis history is public; margin and ADL events are not | Moderate for small positive carry only when spreads are wide; low as a growth engine |
| Staking and stablecoin lending | Quoted 2-6%; mean-reverts to risk-free | Roughly T-bill-like, with protocol risk | Rate history yes; loss events no | High for earning something; no trading edge to validate |
| Passive LP, concentrated | Peer-reviewed evidence says most large pools lose to LVR; some niches positive | Negative expected in volatile pairs; low positive at best in correlated or stable pairs | Yes, with on-chain event data, but substantial build cost | Low |
| Cross-sectional momentum or reversal, long-only top-50 | Sample-dependent, conflicting, costs erase it in liquid names | Likely weaker than published | Yes, but high multiple-testing risk | Low |
| Memecoin entry-signal bots, copy trading, sniping | No signal shown to beat costs; leaderboards contaminated; insiders and snipers extract | Negative expected | Only as a loss-measurement instrument; fills must be modelled with sandwiches and non-landing | Very low |
| CEX-DEX or Solana arbitrage, CEX market making | Concentrated in professional searchers and builders | Not accessible | Not meaningfully | Very low |

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "which strategy makes money" to "which strategy can a small account verify it is not losing money on". The honest ranking is that three families have a real, if modest, chance (slow ensemble trend on majors, carry when spreads are wide, and basic yield), while the strategies most heavily marketed to retail (memecoin sniping, copy trading, DEX LP on volatile pairs) have independent evidence against them. A notable asymmetry is that the credible strategies are the dullest and least testable by a short paper run: trend rules trade only a few dozen times a year, so even several years of daily data give few independent trades, and carry risk shows up in rare events a backtest barely samples. Expect validation to produce a wide confidence interval, not a verdict.

The most defensible bot design therefore combines a conservative trend or carry core with a measurement layer: record every quote and fill, log every configuration tried, compare each result against buy-and-hold and a T-bill yield, and require that a result survive walk-forward testing and a deflated-Sharpe correction before any live money. A small account should price the first months as the cost of learning, and treat any backtest or paper result that beats the roughly 0-2% monthly planning range as a signal to hunt for an unrealistic fill, a survivorship bias or an overfit parameter. This report rests on search-result summaries rather than primary documents, so figures such as the Zarattini et al. Sharpe, the carry yields and the retail failure rates should be re-verified at source before anything is built or funded on them.
